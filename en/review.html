DragonSword: Awakening Review

A gacha game that ditched gacha — and it works. $30 buys you a complete anime action RPG with 19 heroes, deep tag-combat and a surprisingly good story. Not perfect, but one of the best values in the genre.

⭐ Metacritic: 80/100 👍 Steam: ~88% Positive 💰 $29.99
⚡ Quick Verdict

8/10 — Worth buying, especially on sale. DragonSword: Awakening is the rare "gacha game without gacha." For $30 you get a complete anime action RPG with 19 heroes, deep tag-team combat, open world exploration and 150-200 hours of content. The combat is genuinely fun, the story is surprisingly good, and there are zero microtransactions. Downsides: technical roughness, repetitive side content, and limited multiplayer. If you like Genshin Impact but hate gacha, this is your game.

✅ What's Great

1. No Gacha, No P2W

The single most praised feature. One-time $30 purchase, zero microtransactions. All 19 heroes, all gear, all materials — everything is unlocked through gameplay. The only "RNG" is standard RPG crafting RNG (praying for Crit Damage substats). As one Steam reviewer put it: "NO P2W GACHA HELL."

2. Excellent Combat System

  • Fluid, responsive and surprisingly technical
  • Tag-switching and Signal Skill system adds real depth
  • 19 heroes, each with unique playstyles
  • Combo system rewards experimentation
  • Rewards timing and positioning, not button-mashing

NoobFeed: "Combat is fluid, responsive, and unexpectedly technical. It rewards timing and positioning rather than button mashing."

3. Strong Anime Visuals

  • Anime-style art direction
  • Unreal Engine 5
  • Dense open world
  • Excellent character designs
  • Solid optimization for a new studio

4. Surprisingly Good Story

For a game originally designed as a live-service title, the story quality is unexpectedly high. Classic fantasy setting with distinct character personalities — Johnny is notably funny, Lute is the classic anime protagonist. GamingTrend: "Feels like Genshin Impact with the gacha garbage removed, strung together with a fun, interesting story."

5. Content-Rich

  • 19 collectible heroes
  • Gear farming and reforging
  • Raid and dungeon farming
  • Long-term progression systems (Karma, Awakening)
  • Open world exploration with 13 Statues of Organa
  • 150-200 hours for full completion

❌ What's Not Great

1. Technical Roughness

  • Various bugs and technical issues at launch
  • Small-studio jank is noticeable
  • Camera can cause problems in combat
  • Most issues expected to be patched over time

2. Repetitive Content

  • Exploration can feel repetitive
  • Side quests feel like "checklist" content (leftover from MMO design)
  • Side quest rewards may not feel compelling
  • GamesCreed: "Side quests become repetitive"

3. Story Pacing

  • Story can drag in places
  • Clear traces of its MMO/live-service origins

4. Combat Impact

  • Sometimes lacks the "weight" promised by flashy animations
  • Hit feedback could be better

5. Limited Multiplayer

  • Only Hunts (2P) and Raids (3P) support co-op
  • No open-world multiplayer
  • No PvP
  • Cumbersome lobby system with no quick retry

6. New Game = Growing Pains

  • Released July 23, 2026 — still very new
  • Endgame content is limited compared to live-service competitors
  • Waiting on roadmap updates for more content

🎯 Who Should Play This?

✅ Recommended For

  • Genshin Impact fans who hate gacha
  • Anime-style action RPG fans
  • Hero collection and progression lovers
  • Tag-team combat system enthusiasts
  • Players wanting a complete $30 game
  • Open world exploration fans

❌ Not Recommended For

  • Players wanting open-world co-op
  • PvP seekers
  • Zero-tolerance for bugs
  • Players who dislike repetitive content
  • Those expecting AAA-level polish

🏆 Final Verdict

8/10

Great — Worth Buying

DragonSword: Awakening is a flawed but excellent action RPG. Its biggest selling point is the "gacha experience without gacha" — $30 buys you 19 heroes, deep combat and 150+ hours with zero microtransactions. Combat has depth, art is great, story is surprisingly good. Downsides are technical jank, repetitive side content and limited multiplayer. For anime RPG fans who hate gacha, this is currently one of the best options on the market.
